Bill Jia on TPUs, Agentic Kernels, and the AI Build-Out

Notes from a fireside chat with Bill Jia, VP of Engineering for Core ML/AI at Google. Each section separates what Bill said from background context.

SGLang fireside chat · Structured notes · Nov 2026

Speaker: Bill Jia, VP Eng, Core ML/AI, Google (previously Meta, where he led AI infra including PyTorch)
Notes: paraphrased, not verbatim

TL;DR

  1. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is now a core part of how infrastructure gets built. Bill’s answer to “one free week of GCP” was to use agents to write kernels, then use the speedup to win back more compute. He said the same loop is speeding up chip design (layout, simulators, firmware, compilers) and model releases.
  2. Google decided to sell TPUs to the industry, not keep them in-house. After internal debate that reached Sundar Pichai and the board, Google aligned about 1.5 years ago on selling TPUs outside the company. Bill’s view is that the industry needs competition, and that matters more than Google’s revenue. The main gap now is the software ecosystem, not chip performance.
  3. The TPU’s strength is scale. 64-chip ICI cubes are joined by optical circuit switches into 9,216-chip (Ironwood) or 9,600-chip (TPU 8t) superpods. The Virgo network stretches this toward 1M chips, and all the network management is handled in software.
  4. Training and inference silicon have split. TPU 8t targets training. TPU 8i targets inference, with 3× more on-chip SRAM to hold the KV cache, a Boardfly topology, and a collectives engine that replaces SparseCore. TPU 8i Max puts two dies in one package. TPU 8tx adds AMD EPYC hosts for confidential computing.
  5. Bill thinks we are underbuilding. He placed AI at about where the internet was in 1997. Coding agents work, but he said the “at your fingertips” consumer products haven’t appeared yet.

One free week of GCP#

Host: What would you do with one free week of GCP resources?

What Bill said

Use this one week to generate more resources.

  • With enough focus, performance can always be improved.
  • He sees the ultimate way to write kernels as removing the middle abstraction layers and having agents write kernels directly, which is RSI, or ML improving ML.
  • This takes a lot more compute up front. The kernel speedups then free up chips that can go to other work.

Context

The TPU’s kernel language is Pallas, a JAX extension that targets both TPU and GPU. SGLang’s TPU backends run on Pallas kernels written by RadixArk, Google, and the community. This is the layer Bill expects agents to take over. Google has already used AI in hardware design with AlphaChip, which helped lay out the floorplans of recent TPU generations.

Humans vs. agents in the inference stack#

Host: How should responsibility be split between humans and agents? Do we still need people in the inference stack?

What Bill said

  • Humans and agents need to work in a coordinated way.
  • Agents are faster at narrow, well-defined tasks. Humans still set direction and connect the pieces.

From PyTorch to TPU, and opening the TPU to the industry#

Host: You spent years on PyTorch at Meta. Is the TPU real, and why hasn’t it reached the wider community?

What Bill said

  • He spent about 14 years at Meta, where his work included PyTorch. Friends and former coworkers ask him whether the TPU is “a real thing.”
  • His answer: inside Google, pretraining, mid-training, and RL all run on TPU. “100%, not 99%.”
  • Selling TPU as an industry AI-infrastructure product was very controversial. It was argued internally with Sundar Pichai and other VPs, and there were many views inside Google.
  • About 1.5 years ago, leadership, including board directors, agreed to sell TPU to the AI industry and not keep it Google-only.
  • His reasoning: “Google revenue is a smaller thing. The industry needs competition, and that’s the bigger thing.”
  • He said he made “some serious mistakes,” mainly around how much Google’s TPU software was built for internal use.
  • Where things stand: TPU performance is good, but the ecosystem isn’t there yet.

Context

Google has moved quickly since. It announced the Anthropic deal (up to 1M TPUs, more than 1 GW) in Oct 2025, and through 2026 moved toward selling TPU hardware for customers’ own data centers, with reported talks with Meta and others. The software stack has opened up as well: TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend built with Meta, entered preview in April 2026 and was open-sourced in October 2026.

TPU vs. GPU: what you get for onboarding#

Host: If I want to onboard TPU and try it, what extra steps do I need, and what advantage do I get?

Context

Google has released a new TPU about every one to two years since 2015, and the names can be hard to follow: Trillium is v6e, and Ironwood is TPU7x. The rest of this post mostly compares Ironwood with the TPU 8 family.

Training: scale is the main advantage#

What Bill said

  • TPU handles very large pretraining runs well.
  • ICI (Inter-Chip Interconnect) gives low latency and high bandwidth inside a 64-chip cube, which is one rack wired as a 3D torus.
  • OCS (Optical Circuit Switching) joins cubes into one superpod of 9,216 chips (Ironwood) or 9,600 chips (TPU 8t).
  • High-performance software handles all network connection management.
  • Beyond a single superpod, you can connect up to 1M TPUs, with the networking managed automatically. This is very useful for large-scale pretraining.

Context

An Ironwood rack holds 16 hosts × 4 chips, which forms one 64-chip cube wired as a 3D torus over ICI. Each chip links to 6 neighbors at about 1.2 TB/s bidirectional. An Ironwood (TPU7x) superpod joins 144 cubes into 9,216 chips; a TPU 8t superpod reaches 9,600. Beyond the pod, Google’s new Virgo network, together with JAX and Pathways, gives near-linear scaling up to 1M chips in one logical cluster: about 134k per data center and 1M across sites.

Inference: Tensor Cores, SparseCores, and memory#

Bill named two on-chip engines, the TensorCore and the SparseCore. Here is what each does and which workloads benefit.

TensorCoreSparseCore
What it isLarge systolic arrays (MXUs) for dense matmul. The vector unit handles softmax, norms, and activations, and the scalar unit handles control flow. This is where the FLOPs are.Dataflow processors for irregular gathers and scatters into huge tables, a pattern the MXU handles poorly. Introduced in TPU v4.
TrainingNearly all pretraining compute (GEMMs in attention and FFN, both forward and backward) runs here.Embedding-heavy models such as recommenders and ranking. Per the v4 paper, SparseCores speed up these models 5–7× while using only about 5% of die area and power.
InferencePrefill is compute-bound and fills the MXUs. Decode is memory-bound, so speed depends on how fast weights and KV cache reach the MXUs. That’s why on-chip SRAM matters.Embedding lookups for ads, search, and retrieval. LLM decode makes little use of them, which is why TPU 8i swaps them for a collectives engine aimed at MoE all-to-all traffic.

Memory hierarchy: why kernels port differently#

What Bill said

  • NVIDIA GPUs have a complex, tiered memory hierarchy.
  • The TPU is built around a large on-chip SRAM, with no tiered transfers to manage.
  • If a kernel is written well for TPU, inference performance can improve substantially. He mentioned SGLang + TorchTPU.

Context

TPUs still have off-chip HBM (192 GB on Ironwood, 288 GB on TPU 8i), and NVIDIA data-center GPUs stack registers, L1/shared memory, and L2 above their HBM. The key difference is who manages the on-chip memory. On a GPU, L1 and L2 are partly hardware-managed caches. On a TPU, VMEM is a software-managed scratchpad: the compiler (XLA) or the kernel author (Pallas) schedules every DMA between HBM and SRAM explicitly. That makes performance predictable, but it puts the burden on the compiler or kernel author, which is the opening for agent-written kernels. TPU 8i increases this SRAM to 384 MB per chip, sized for the KV cache of reasoning models.

Context

  • TorchTPU is a native, eager-first PyTorch backend for TPU, built by Google and Meta. It replaces PyTorch/XLA. Preview was April 2026, and it was open-sourced in October 2026 with PyTorch-native vLLM and SGLang backends.
  • SGLang-Jax is the current TPU path for SGLang. It supports Gemma, Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi, MiniMax, and Grok, among others.
  • RadixArk × Google (Jul 2026) adds SGL-torchtpu with eager mode, MPMD, Radix Cache, HiCache, and speculative decoding. It also promises day-0 TPU support for new open models.

Software serves as an open bridge rather than a lock-in mechanism … effectively eliminating the ‘migration tax’ for developers.

Bill Jia, LMSYS blog, Jul 30 2026

The TPU 8 family: training and inference split#

Host: What’s the engineering reasoning behind TPU 8t and TPU 8i?

What Bill said

  • Training and inference workloads will keep moving further apart. In the past, one chip served both. Starting with TPU 8, they use different chips.
  • TPU 8i has much more SRAM, because inference needs very short latency, especially for multi-turn agentic workloads. It keeps active parameters and weights on-chip, cuts network latency for distributed inference, and uses Arm-based Axion host CPUs.
  • Many customers want confidential computing, so Google added TPU 8tx, which uses AMD EPYC hosts and builds on AMD’s investment in confidential CPU computing.
  • TPU 8t is for training.
  • TPU 8i Max puts two chips in one package with very high bandwidth between them. SRAM and HBM are shared, so the combined unit has more resources and performs much better.
  • Agents now help with chip design and low-level design. Chip design is moving much faster, and the TPU roadmap is far quicker than a few years ago.

Context

TPU 8t and TPU 8i were announced at Google Cloud Next on Apr 22, 2026. TPU 8i Max and TPU 8tx followed on Nov 9, 2026. TPU 8tx pairs 8t silicon with 5th-gen AMD EPYC hosts that use SEV to extend confidential computing to the accelerator. The base 8t and 8i use Google’s Arm-based Axion hosts.

Why model and chip releases are speeding up#

Q: Model release cycles have shortened a lot. What’s driving the faster pace?

What Bill said

Using code to improve code (RSI) applies to chip design too, because chip design is also code. It has two parts:

  1. Physical layout: transistors, connections, network layout, and chipsets. Coding agents help a lot here.
  2. Simulators, low-level kernels, firmware, drivers, and compilers: coding agents have sped these up greatly, so chips reach production faster.

On models:

  • Anthropic started RSI first, and Google was slower. Recent Gemini releases are coming faster, which suggests RSI is starting to work.
  • Very large-scale pretraining is still valuable: models are getting more complex, there’s more data, and vertical enterprise data is a big opportunity.
  • The pattern: get a strong base model out first (Gemini 4 “Argon”). Additional fine-tuning then produces “Carbon” only weeks later. Run the big pretraining, release the large model, then iterate with RSI.
  • Then distill and quantize to get Flash models. He expects future releases to follow this pattern, and said “we are far from the end of the scaling law.”

Context

Gemini 4 “Argon” reached general availability on Oct 13, 2026 as gemini-4-argon. “Carbon” is a newer Gemini 4 checkpoint reported to be in internal testing; Google hasn’t detailed its relationship to Argon. Gemini 3.5 Pro was skipped in favor of Flash models and Gemini 4.

Overbuilding, or still not enough data centers?#

Q: Data centers and compute are being built out fast (SpaceX among others), and demand for compute and tokens keeps rising. Will we end up overbuilding in the next couple of years?

What Bill said

  • He called it a subjective answer, then said he’s a strong believer in more build-out.
  • The internet analogy: from about 1995 we went from modems to DSL to broadband, and now Wi-Fi is taken for granted. Early on, it was far worse.
  • AI really started with GPT-3, and before GPT-3.5 everything was at a different stage. AI today is roughly the internet in 1997. Think how much happened between 1997 and 2026.
  • Coding agents are great. Consumer assistants like Meta’s Muse and ByteDance’s Doubao are “OK,” but there are no meaningful “at your fingertips” products yet. Will there be? “100%.” The real AI demand hasn’t arrived.
  • His view: we are significantly underbuilding.

Context

This matches what Bill said in a late-September 2026 interview: AI demand can still grow “10×, 100×, 1,000×,” power is the binding constraint on next year’s build-out, and inference may already have overtaken training. Muse refers to Meta’s Muse Spark (Apr 2026). Doubao is ByteDance’s assistant.
